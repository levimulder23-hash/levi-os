### 1. FIE London — Program Specifics

**Location**: Foundation House, 114 Cromwell Road, London SW7 4ES — South Kensington. Walking distance to Natural History Museum, V&A, Hyde Park, Royal Albert Hall, and Chelsea's King's Road. The Tube stop is South Kensington (Piccadilly/Circle/District lines).

**Housing**: FIE places students in residences. Expect to be in or near Kensington/South Ken based on program center location. Specific housing revealed in the FIE questionnaire (available early August at studyabroad.fie.org.uk).

**Film-relevant courses** (from the current FIE London catalogue):

Key Course (most film students take this):
- **British Life and Visual Media** — the flagship course that contextualizes media in British life; essentially required unless your home institution specifies otherwise

Electives worth knowing about:
- **Underdogs and Anti-Heroes: The Social Realist Tradition in British Cinema** — the most directly film-centric elective; covers directors like Ken Loach, Mike Leigh, Andrea Arnold
- **Contemporary British Broadcasting** — TV/streaming landscape in the UK; relevant for industry knowledge
- **Media in Britain** — broader media industry course
- **Photojournalism: London through the Lens** — requires your own camera; co-curricular field shoots; excellent for cinematographers
- **Theatre in Britain: From the Globe to the West End** — £110 lab fee; field visits to productions; strong for screenwriters
- **'Cash, Money, Records Forever': An Introduction to the Business Practices of the Evolving British Music Industry** — cross-disciplinary, useful if interested in music video/documentary
- **The Nature of Story: Creative Writing** — good for screenwriting foundation

**Internship program**: FIE offers a Study & Internship program (separate track with corresponding Experiential Education Course). If you're on the standard LMU London semester, check with FIE directly whether you can add a part-time internship placement. Film/media internships in London via FIE have historically been available in production companies, broadcasters, and post-production houses. This is one of the biggest career opportunities of the semester — chase it aggressively. Contact: studyabroad@fie.org.uk.

**Typical week structure**: Most students take 4-5 courses. FIE schedules classes Mon-Fri with field visits woven in. Afternoons and weekends are largely free. FIE arranges cultural excursions; participation varies.

### 2. UK Banking & Money

**Pre-departure setup (open before you leave)**:
- **Wise** (wise.com) — best starting option for Americans. Open from US with American ID and address. Get a UK account number + sort code immediately. Physical Wise card ~$10 one-time fee. Mid-market exchange rate, ~0.63% conversion fee. 2 free ATM withdrawals/month (up to £200 equivalent). Accepts 40+ currencies. Wise has a dedicated student account page (wise.com/gb/students). Parents can send money cheaply this way too. ✅ Open this first.
- **Revolut** — similar to Wise, also openable from US. Slightly different fee structure. Fine alternative or supplement.
- **Charles Schwab High-Yield Checking** — no foreign transaction fees, reimburses ATM fees worldwide, FDIC insured. If you already have Schwab, keep using it in London. The gold standard for American travelers.

**After arrival (once you have a UK address from FIE housing)**:
- **Starling Bank** — best UK bank for students. No monthly fees, no ATM fees globally, real-time notifications, excellent budgeting app. Ranked #2 in UK service quality (77% recommend). Apply online; requires UK residency and 18+. Open once you have FIE housing as proof of address.
- **Monzo** — similar to Starling, #1 in UK service quality (82%). Also requires UK address. The TOTUM card you get as a student links well with Monzo's budgeting "Pots" feature.
- **High-street banks** (Barclays, Lloyds, HSBC, NatWest) — straightforward once you have a UK address, but slower to open. Not necessary if you have Starling/Monzo.

**FATCA note**: Being American means some UK banks are reluctant due to IRS reporting requirements. Major banks and all fintechs listed above are fine. If you open a UK account with £10k+ at any point, technically FBAR filing applies — but this is just an information filing, not a tax.

**Wise vs. Schwab strategy**: Use Schwab debit for ATM withdrawals (free worldwide). Use Wise or Starling for day-to-day spending to get local bank rates without fees. Keep a US bank account open the whole time.

### 3. UK SIM Card

**Best options for 4 months**:

- **Giffgaff** ⭐ — top pick for students. £10/month rolling plan, 100GB data (student deal — requires .ac.uk email; check if FIE/LMU provides one, or if LMU email qualifies), unlimited UK calls/texts, 5GB EU roaming. No contract, cancel anytime. Order free SIM online at giffgaff.com/students — arrives next day by post. This is the best value in the UK by a wide margin if you qualify. Standard non-student plan: £10/month for 20GB; still solid.
- **Three** — most affordable unlimited data. ~£12/month (first 6 months) or ~£18/month thereafter. Good 5G coverage. Rolling monthly contract, cancel anytime. Can pick up SIM at Heathrow Terminals 2 & 3 or T5 upon arrival.
- **Smarty** (Three network) — £20/month unlimited data including hotspot. Cancel anytime. Great for heavy data users.

**Order before you leave**: Giffgaff SIM ships to US addresses — order now, forward to yourself, or order to a UK address when you have one. Otherwise Three is available at Heathrow on arrival.

**On arrival at Heathrow**: Three, EE, and O2 all have kiosks/shops in the terminals (Terminals 2-3 primarily). You can get a working UK SIM within 30 minutes of landing.

**Note on EU roaming**: If you're traveling Europe on weekends, confirm your UK SIM's EU roaming terms before crossing the Channel. Giffgaff includes 5GB EU roaming on most plans. Smarty includes unlimited EU data. Three has "Go Roam" in 71 countries.

### 4. Transportation

**Oyster vs. Contactless**:
The short answer in 2025-2026: **just use your Starling/Monzo/Wise debit card or Apple Pay**. Contactless cards and Oyster are charged at identical rates, and both benefit from the automatic daily/weekly price cap. You do not need an Oyster card unless you're applying for the 18+ Student Oyster.

**18+ Student Oyster photocard**: Available to students enrolled in London institutions. Gives 30% off adult-rate Travelcards and 1/3 off off-peak Tube fares on Oyster. Costs £20 admin fee. Apply via tfl.gov.uk once your FIE enrollment is confirmed. If you're commuting daily, this pays off fast.

**16-25 Railcard** ⭐ (for UK-wide travel): £35/year or £80/3 years. 1/3 off standard UK rail fares. Average saving: £208/year. Also gives 1/3 off off-peak Oyster fares if you link it to your Oyster card. **Essential for weekend trips to Edinburgh, Bath, Oxford, Cambridge, Brighton, etc.** Buy digitally at railcard.co.uk — available in the Railcard app.

**Heathrow → Central London**:
- **Elizabeth line**: £13.90 to Zone 1, ~45 min, runs every 10-15 min. Best overall. Stops include Paddington, Tottenham Court Road, Liverpool Street.
- **Tube (Piccadilly line)**: From £5.50, ~50-60 min, runs frequently. Cheapest option — just slow and no luggage space.
- **Heathrow Express**: 15 min to Paddington, £10+ booked advance (£25+ on the day). Fast but expensive and not worth it unless you're very late.
- **National Express coach**: £10 to Victoria, ~1-1.5 hrs. Cheapest but slowest.

Recommendation on arrival day: **Piccadilly line** (cheapest and direct; just drag your bags). Once you have a UK card: Elizabeth line is the comfort choice.

**Navigation apps**:
- **Citymapper** — use this. Londoners swear by it. Better than Google Maps for real-time bus updates, walking routes, bike share options, and complex multi-modal journeys.
- **Google Maps** — fine as backup; Citymapper is better for London specifically.

### 5. Cost of Living + Budget

**Realistic monthly budget (London, housing covered by FIE)**:
- Groceries: £100-150 (Lidl, Aldi, Tesco budget range; avoid Waitrose/M&S for daily shopping)
- Transport: £67 avg; with Student Oyster + 16-25 Railcard combination: potentially £40-50 for local
- Going out/socializing: £60-100
- Eating out: £50-80 (London has incredible £5-8 lunch deals; avoid central tourist restaurants)
- Misc (clothes, subscriptions, personal care): £50-80
- **Total est. (housing covered): £350-500/month conservative; £600-800 with active social life**

**Cheap eating strategy**:
- Supermarket meal deals: ~£3.50 (sandwich + crisps + drink) from Boots, Sainsbury's, Tesco — the working lunch of London
- Wetherspoons pubs: cheap food and drinks, everywhere
- Dishoom (Covent Garden/Shoreditch) for the experience — not cheap but worth one visit
- Borough Market on Friday/Saturday — expensive produce but free samples
- Brick Lane for cheap Indian and bagels (Beigel Bake is legendary, 24/7, ~£1)
- Leon, Pret A Manger, Itsu for fast casual
- Lidl and Aldi are significantly cheaper than Tesco/Sainsbury's — worth the trip

**Student discounts** — get all of these:
- **UNiDAYS** (free) — Apple, ASOS, Nike, H&M, many restaurants. Use your LMU .edu email.
- **Student Beans** (free) — similar, good for additional brands
- **TOTUM** (£12/year) — discount at thousands of UK stores in-person and online, including ASOS, PrettyLittleThing, Deliveroo; the only card that works in-store on high street
- **ISIC card** — international student ID, discounts in 130+ countries; essential for European travel (museums, transport)
- **16-25 Railcard** (£35/year) — non-negotiable if you're taking any UK train trips
- **Amazon Prime Student** — 6 months free, then 50% off
- **Spotify** — 50% off student rate (~£5.99/month vs £11.99)
- **Deliveroo Plus** — 1 year free

### 6. Film Industry in London

**Key geography**:
- **Soho** (W1) — the historical heart of London's film industry: production companies, casting agents, post houses, talent agencies, BAFTA headquarters (195 Piccadilly, just south of Soho). Wardour Street is historically "film alley." Dean Street, Greek Street, and Frith Street have agencies and production offices throughout.
- **Shoreditch/East London** — creative and media hub; indie production companies, VFX studios, advertising agencies
- **South Bank** — BFI Southbank (NFT), BFI IMAX; riverside walk between Waterloo and London Bridge is a creative corridor
- **Shepperton Studios** (Surrey) and **Pinewood Studios** (Buckinghamshire) — major production studios; day trips possible, sometimes open for events

**Key institutions for networking/events**:
- **BFI (British Film Institute)** — BFI Southbank has year-round screenings, talks, retrospectives; membership gives film access. **BFI London Film Festival** is typically held mid-October — likely Oct 2026 (dates TBD). Industry Forum has press/industry passes; student/emerging filmmaker access is possible. Sign up for BFI mailing list at bfi.org.uk.
- **BAFTA** — 195 Piccadilly. BAFTA Connect is their membership tier for emerging/mid-level creatives (access to screenings, networking, events). BAFTA runs talent programs, Q&As, and panels throughout the year. Check bafta.org/events.
- **London Film School** (LFS, Covent Garden) — short courses, alumni events, occasional public screenings; worth exploring
- **Film London** — city's film industry body; events and networking for filmmakers
- **Raindance Film Festival** — typically September/October, London; major indie festival with workshops and networking
- **FIE internship pipeline** — FIE has existing relationships with London media companies; push hard for this

**How to break in as a visiting student**:
- Attend BFI free and paid screenings, then join the Q&As — directors/producers answer questions after
- BAFTA Q&A screenings (members get first access; student rates often available)
- Volunteer at film festivals (BFI LFF typically needs volunteers — apply in August/September)
- LinkedIn search for Soho production companies; cold email for informational coffee
- The FIE "London overlap" noted in your existing notes is real — exploit it

### 7. Things to Do / Weekend Travel

**London essentials** (Kensington is already great — you'll live near most of these):
- V&A Museum (literally minutes from FIE) — world's greatest design/art museum, free
- Natural History Museum (same block) — free, spectacular building
- Hyde Park / Kensington Gardens — daily habit
- Tate Modern (Bankside) — free, incredible permanent collection
- National Gallery (Trafalgar Square) — free; some of the greatest paintings on earth
- Borough Market (London Bridge) — go hungry
- Portobello Road Market (Notting Hill, Saturday) — antiques, vintage, food
- Columbia Road Flower Market (Bethnal Green, Sunday morning) — a visual feast
- Street art, Shoreditch / Brick Lane
- West End theatre — standbys and TKTS same-day discount booth in Leicester Square; or rush tickets from theatre box offices (from £20)
- Hampstead Heath / Primrose Hill — for a London skyline view
- Electric Cinema (Notting Hill) — independent cinema, reclining armchairs, worth it
- Camden Market
- Greenwich by boat down the Thames

**Weekend trips from London** ⭐:
- **Paris**: Eurostar St Pancras → Gare du Nord, ~2h15min. Book 3-4 weeks ahead for £50-80 each way. Iconic.
- **Amsterdam**: Eurostar direct, ~3h50min. Cheapest fares from £50 each way. Do it.
- **Edinburgh**: Train ~4.5h (London King's Cross → Edinburgh Waverley); 16-25 Railcard brings price down significantly. Or fly: easyJet/Ryanair from Stansted/Gatwick ~£30-60 RT. Edinburgh in October is particularly special (Samhuinn/Halloween).
- **Bath**: 1.5h from Paddington; some of the best Roman history in the UK + beautiful Georgian architecture. Easy day trip or overnight.
- **Brighton**: 1h from Victoria; seaside, independent shops, great food scene. Perfect for a sunny weekend.
- **Bruges, Belgium**: Eurostar to Brussels (~2h), then 1h by train to Bruges. Fairy-tale medieval city.
- **Reykjavik**: Cheap flights from London; worth it for a long weekend if you can swing it

**Alps skiing (Ikon Pass)**:
- Route: Eurostar London St Pancras → Paris Gare du Nord (2h15), walk or Metro to Paris Gare de Lyon, TGV → Alps (Bourg-Saint-Maurice ~5.5h total from Paris; Chambery ~3h from Paris; Grenoble ~3h)
- Ikon Pass resorts in French Alps: Tignes/Val d'Isère (connected to Bourg-Saint-Maurice via funicular to Les Arcs), Les Arcs, La Plagne, Sainte Foy Tarentaise
- Ikon Pass Swiss Alps: Verbier (Eurostar → Paris → TGV to Martigny → train to Le Châble)
- **Easier route**: Fly. London Gatwick/Heathrow → Geneva (~1.5h, £50-100), then bus/transfer to Alps. Often cheaper and faster door-to-door.
- **Timing**: Alps ski season starts late November/December. Your fall term likely ends Dec 2026, so a ski trip at end-of-semester is viable. Plan early.
- Note: The "Eurostar Snow Train" (direct London→Bourg-Saint-Maurice) is/was a seasonal service — check status as of 2026. The standard Eurostar+TGV connection always works.

**Packing**:
- **Type G plug adapters** (3 rectangular pins; UK is different from EU). Bring 2-3 adapters. UK voltage is 230V — US electronics that are dual voltage (100-240V, check the label) only need an adapter, not a converter. Laptops, phone chargers, and most modern electronics are dual voltage.
- Pack light for clothes — you'll buy things in London and it's easy to ship back. Season: August-December = layers; London is rainy, chilly by October.
- Bring a good rain jacket/shell. Umbrella is optional (Londoners consider umbrellas a tourist tell, but they're practical).

**Medications** (see Crohn's section below for specifics):
- Stock up on ALL prescriptions before leaving — aim for 5-month supply. The NHS does not cover non-residents for free prescriptions (you'd pay the standard NHS prescription charge ~£9.90/item per prescription, but supply continuity is the bigger issue).
- Bring medications in original labeled pharmacy bottles.
- Bring your physician's letter (on letterhead) stating your diagnoses, medications, and dosages — in English.

**Money**:
- Arrive with some £GBP cash (~£100) for first day needs: tube ticket, food, immediate expenses
- Best way to get GBP: use Schwab or Wise ATM once landed (not airport currency exchange kiosks — terrible rates)

### 9. Crohn's — Travel-Specific Considerations

> This is flagged as requiring extra care. Not medical advice — coordinate with your GI doctor before departure.

**Travel insurance** ⚠️:
- **Standard travel insurance will not cover Crohn's-related treatment abroad**, even policies that claim "pre-existing condition" coverage. Read the fine print on biologic medications specifically — regular policies typically exclude them.
- Recommended insurers for study abroad with IBD: IMG Global (GlobeHopper), GeoBlue (specifically designed for study abroad), HTH Worldwide, Seven Corners. Compare policies specifically for pre-existing IBD coverage.
- Check whether LMU's mandatory study abroad insurance covers Crohn's — it may not at the level you need. You may need supplemental coverage.
- IBD Passport (ibdpassport.com) has specific guidance on travel insurance for IBD — read their "Health Care Abroad" section before choosing a policy.
- Crohn's & Colitis UK charity: crohnsandcolitis.org.uk — they have a helpline and resources for people with IBD in the UK.

**Medications**:
- If you're on a biologic (Humira, Stelara, Skyrizi, Entyvio, etc.): these require special handling (some need refrigeration), a doctor's letter for customs, and careful supply planning. Humira and Adalimumab (generic) are available in the UK but supply must be arranged in advance.
- If you're on mesalazine, steroids, or immunomodulators: stock up for the full 4-5 months. Easier to bring from the US than navigate UK prescriptions without registered NHS patient status.
- Carry medications in carry-on luggage — never checked bags.
- Get a doctor's letter (on letterhead, in English) describing your condition and all medications. Present at customs if needed.

**Finding GI care in London**:
- Register with an NHS GP (General Practitioner) as a temporary resident near your FIE housing. This is free and gives you access to emergency/urgent care. GPs can refer you to NHS gastroenterology.
- **St. Mark's Hospital** (Harrow, NW London) — one of the world's leading specialist gut hospitals; particularly renowned for IBD. If you need specialist GI care, this is the best in the UK.
- **University College London Hospital** (UCLH) — gastroenterology department; central London
- **King's College Hospital** (Denmark Hill) — strong IBD center
- **Imperial College Healthcare** (multiple sites, West London — closer to Kensington)
- Ask FIE staff about their recommended healthcare contacts for students with chronic conditions; good programs have this information on hand.

**Day-to-day living with Crohn's in London**:
- **RADAR Key** (~£5, available on Amazon UK): unlocks accessible/disabled toilets throughout the UK (train stations, shopping centers, etc.). Invaluable during flares. Get one once you arrive.
- Apps: **Toilet Map UK**, **Flush** (toilet finder app) — map out accessible facilities in areas you frequent
- London has good dietary awareness — restaurants often list ingredients; "free from" sections in supermarkets; vegetarian/vegan/gluten-free options widely available
- Pack snacks for travel days (trains, day trips, airports)
